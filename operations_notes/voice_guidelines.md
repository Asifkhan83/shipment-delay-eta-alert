# Apex Transit Solutions — Communication and Email Voice Guide

Apex Transit Solutions is a mid-sized regional fulfillment center (sample company, used for practice). We are launching an automated vendor dispatch and inventory reporting system.

**Who we write to:**
- B2B retail clients: delay alerts, ETA updates, back-on-schedule follow-ups
- Suppliers and vendors: dispatch requests, dock appointments, inventory reports
- Internal operations managers: critical delay escalations, daily summaries

**How every reader should feel:** informed and in control. They know what happened, what we're doing about it, and what (if anything) they need to do.

---

## 1. Our operational tone in three words

**Factual. Calm. Clear.**

- **Factual:** state only what we know, and where we learned it.
- **Calm:** no drama, no hype, one short apology at most.
- **Clear:** exact dates, plain words, one obvious next step.

## 2. Logistics terminology we use

**Rule:** in client emails, spell out an abbreviation the first time it appears, for example "bill of lading (BOL)".

**All emails**
- ETA (estimated time of arrival)
- Revised ETA (the new ETA after a delay)
- Delivery window (the agreed time range for delivery)
- BOL (bill of lading: the shipping document listing what's on the truck)
- POD (proof of delivery: signed confirmation the goods arrived)
- SKU (the unique code for each product)
- Tracking ID
- In transit
- Out for delivery

**Vendor and supplier emails (in addition)**
- ASN (advance shipping notice: the vendor's "stock is on its way" notice)
- Dock appointment (a booked time slot to load or unload)
- Cut-off time (latest time stock can arrive and still ship that day)
- LTL (less-than-truckload: a shipment that shares a truck)
- FTL (full truckload: one customer's freight fills the truck)

**Internal operations emails only**
- Cross-dock
- Pick and pack
- Cycle count
- PRO number (the carrier's LTL tracking number)

## 3. Terms we avoid

| Avoid | Example to avoid | Write this instead |
|---|---|---|
| Vague timing | "soon", "shortly", "ASAP", "in a few days" | "Your revised ETA is Nov 14." |
| Blame-shifting | "the carrier's fault", "out of our hands", "unforeseen issues" | "Your shipment is delayed. Here's what we're doing about it." |
| Over-apologizing | "we deeply and sincerely regret any inconvenience" | "We're sorry for the delay." |
| Guessing the cause | "probably weather", "likely a mechanical issue" | State only a reason confirmed by an authorized operational source, and name the source. |
| Internal jargon in client emails | "cross-dock", "pick and pack", "WMS glitch" | "our transfer point", "our warehouse" |
| Hype and filler | "world-class", "seamless", "lightning-fast" | Plain facts: "Your delivery is back on schedule." |

## 4. Delivery and inventory claims we must never make

The rule behind all six: **say only what we know right now, and where we learned it. Never promise the future.**

1. **No guaranteed delivery times.** Say "Your revised ETA is Nov 14", never "guaranteed by Nov 14".
2. **No "this won't happen again".** Say what we changed instead, for example "We've added a backup carrier on this route."
3. **No unconfirmed stock levels.** Give the source and date: "Our Nov 9 cycle count shows 500 units on hand."
4. **No claims about the condition of goods before the receiver inspects them.** Say "Delivery is confirmed. The signed POD is attached."
5. **No refunds, credits or compensation in automated emails.** Say "Your account manager will contact you about next steps." A person decides money matters.
6. **No promises on behalf of a carrier or vendor.** Report what they confirmed: "Pickup is scheduled for 9 am tomorrow (confirmed by the carrier)."

## 5. How we sign off automated dispatch emails

Every automated email ends with the team name, a way to reach a human, and an honest note that it was sent automatically. We never sign with a person's name on an automated email.

```
— Apex Transit Solutions Dispatch Team
This update was sent automatically from our tracking system.
Questions? Reply to this email or call (555) 010-0000 (Mon–Fri, 7 am–7 pm).
```

The phone number is a practice placeholder. Do not replace it with a real contact during the course.
